# Speech evidence and uncertain results

Keydown analyzes microphone PCM locally in memory with the macOS SoundAnalysis version-1 classifier. The classifier runs independently of recognition: decoder output and volume alone cannot authorize automatic delivery. No additional model is downloaded, audio is not sent to a service, and analysis creates no temporary recording files.

Toggle dictation and selected-text command execution require completed analysis with two adjacent overlapping speech windows at confidence 0.8 or above. Push-to-dictate also accepts windows at 0.75 or above when an adjacent pair includes a window at 0.8 or above. The baseline gate in both modes allows unqualified spans up to three seconds. Total unqualified duration must be no greater than qualified speech duration plus a pause allowance. The allowance is two seconds for toggle dictation and selected-text edits, and three seconds for a deliberate hold. Prefix, interior and trailing intervals all count. Overlapping qualified windows are counted by their union, not added twice; this prevents repeated small speech bursts from authorizing a mostly uncertain recording. The actual captured duration is checked at completion, including any partial final classifier hop. Missing coverage longer than one hop invalidates eligibility. Analysis uses 0.5-second windows and 50% overlap. This is a conservative application policy, not a claim that model confidence equals accuracy or that every recognized word is correct. It cannot identify which person spoke, distinguish intentional dictation from audible speech playback, or prove transcript fidelity.

Releasing a verified physical hold can also qualify ordinary dictation in three narrow cases. Each requires completed coverage. A single classifier window spanning at least 0.5 seconds with confidence ≥0.95 can qualify one decoded word in a take of at most 0.65 seconds. A take of at most three seconds can qualify when all of at least three consecutive windows score ≥0.75. An interior gap over three and up to six seconds can qualify when supported speech pairs flank it, every intervening speech score is ≤0.3 and every PCM sample in the gap has absolute amplitude ≤0.002. The gap still counts as three seconds toward the overall coverage budget. Long leading and trailing gaps, louder noise, uncertain gaps and missing samples stay reviewable. These exceptions apply only to release of the physical key during ordinary dictation. Editing, practice, toggle and automatic stops use their existing gates.

By default, if speech evidence is uncertain, missing, failed, cancelled or overloaded, recognition is still allowed. The raw result stays available through Review Previous Text. Automatic insertion and editing require stronger speech evidence. Quiet and short utterances remain available for explicit copy; they are not discarded based on loudness. Practice remains unsaved. History and recording retention follow the existing settings, with recording storage off by default. Pure digital silence can still return the existing empty-audio outcome.

The opt-in **Allow short uncertain holds with positive speech evidence** setting lets an explicit hold-key release deliver uncertain text from a short take (up to three seconds of analyzed audio) after successful completion and at least one speech window at confidence 0.8 or higher. A completed analysis of noise alone cannot qualify. Longer recordings must meet the speech criteria above. Destination checks still apply. Toggle, selected-text editing, practice, duration caps and failed or incomplete analysis do not use this option. Background noise can produce unwanted words when this setting is enabled.

Hands-free timing uses classifier audio timestamps, not a startup volume baseline or elapsed callback time. Speech at the beginning is eligible from the first analysis window. Uncertain sound delays silence completion; only speech-confidence intervals at or below 0.3 count as clear silence (the version-1 digital-silence fixture scores approximately 0.204). Hold-to-talk ignores the hands-free silence rule. Manual stop, cancellation, input-stall checks and the duration cap remain available if classification fails or cannot establish speech. Classifier latency can delay hands-free stopping.

The analysis queue holds at most two seconds of pending PCM. Overflow invalidates its authority instead of silently dropping samples and trusting incomplete evidence. Finalization has a three-second delivery deadline; timeout remains review-only. Cancellation invalidates the session's analyzer, and a new recording uses a new analyzer. Each take owns a separate tap ring and converter. The worker drains through the release boundary and finalizes the converter. Transport overflow or a conversion error invalidates evidence for the take. Classification finalization and recognition overlap after release.

Whisper receives no initial text prompt. A local replay of low-level noise with a dictionary prompt produced a dictionary entry as the transcription. The same PCM decoded as blank with an empty prompt. Dictionary spelling still applies during prose cleanup.

## Qualification limits

Synthetic tests cover the historical 60-second noise fixture, changing noise, ordinary and quiet synthesized speech, and speech beginning before any calibration period followed by silence. The standard unit/native suites cover policy, cancellation, buffer limits and recovery UI. Physical microphone, accent, whisper, room-noise, music/voice playback, Bluetooth route and real-time load qualification must still be performed on the user's devices. A general sound classifier can make mistakes; uncertain review is an intentional fallback, not a guarantee against every hallucination.

The 0.2.3 review reproduced invented text after speech followed by a 60-second noise tail. In 0.2.4, that session and its prefix/interior-noise variants require review. Recognition still receives all retained audio, so no onset/tail cropping can discard quiet words. Normal speech and the tested brief ordinary pause remain eligible; long held pauses can deliberately trigger review. These duration thresholds are a conservative delivery policy, not transcript-to-audio alignment. Classifier false positives and hallucinations in shorter eligible intervals remain possible; this is not complete semantic verification.
